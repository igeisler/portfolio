Executive summary
The problem

I was training a GRU to rank roughly 1,000 US stocks by next-day return. In practice, training effectively stopped within the first few epochs. Rather than increase capacity or simply train longer, I wanted to understand why useful learning ended so early, identify the recurrent mechanism responsible, and intervene directly.

Starting model
•

Inputs: one year of history compressed into 14 ordered time buckets, using returns and price dynamics, trading activity, liquidity, volatility and risk, momentum/reversal, market and sector exposures, and firm characteristics.

•

Architecture: one-layer GRU with 64 hidden units and a \(64\rightarrow32\rightarrow1\) prediction head.

•

Loss: for each date, with centred model scores \(a_i=s_i-\bar s\) and standardised next-day return ranks \(u_i\),

\[ L_d = 1- \frac{\frac1N\sum_i a_i u_i} {\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_i a_i^2+10^{-8}}}. \]

The objective is to learn the cross-sectional ordering of returns rather than their noisy magnitudes.

•

Initialisation: Xavier input weights, separately orthogonal recurrent blocks and Kaiming head weights. Biases were zero except for chrono initialisation of the update gate,

\[ b_{iz,j}=\log(\tau_j-1),\qquad \tau_j\sim U(2,T), \]

which gives the GRU an initial bias towards long memory.

What the diagnostics showed

Two findings mattered. First, the update gate copies a share of the previous hidden state, \(h_{t-1}\), straight into the next one: \(z_t\odot h_{t-1}\). Removing only that final copy term raised development Rank IC from 0.0156 to 0.0193. History was useful, but the final prediction relied too heavily on directly carried state.

Second, how much early history still reaches the end is the transport from bucket \(s\) to the final bucket \(T=14\):

\[ P_s=\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial h_s}=J_T\cdots J_{s+1},\qquad J_t=\frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-1}}. \]

Its size says how strongly \(h_s\) still moves \(h_T\); its stable rank indicates how dominant the leading singular direction has become. For the oldest history, transport magnitude grew by almost an order of magnitude while stable rank fell from about 3.9 to 1.4. The recurrence was preserving more historical influence, but the transport was becoming increasingly dominated by its leading singular direction.

Taken together, the diagnostics suggested that the issue was not simply too little memory, but how memory was being carried and transformed through the recurrence. That shifted the investigation from model capacity toward the internal dynamics of state persistence and transport.

Interventions
1

Balanced write and carry. I removed chrono initialisation and started the update gate around \(z=0.5\). Early in training the hidden state is still poorly developed, so it is undesirable to assume that copying it should dominate new input. A balanced starting point keeps the model receptive while allowing memory to emerge as the representation improves.

2

Protect recurrent breadth early. I added a temporary normalised-isotropy penalty to the recurrent weight matrices,

\[ R_W=\sum_g\frac1H \left\| \frac{W_{hg}^{\top}W_{hg}} {\operatorname{tr}(W_{hg}^{\top}W_{hg})/H}-I \right\|_F^2, \]

slowing the rapid development of dominant recurrent directions.

3

Regularise the actual transport. I applied the same idea directly to the composed Jacobians,

\[ R_T=\frac1{|\mathcal S|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\frac1H \left\| \frac{P_s^{\top}P_s} {\operatorname{tr}(P_s^{\top}P_s)/H}-I \right\|_F^2. \]

Both penalties were temporary. The goal was not to keep the network isotropic, but to protect the early representation-building phase before allowing later specialisation.

This last point became the broader design principle. In noisy financial data, the easiest early gradient direction can become dominant quickly. I therefore wanted the first part of training to remain receptive and well-conditioned, giving the GRU time to build a useful hidden representation before optimisation was allowed to specialise it aggressively.

The sections below work through the diagnostics and interventions in full, with the measurements they rest on.

The question

I was training a GRU to rank roughly 1,000 US stocks by next-day return, but training effectively stopped within the first few epochs even as training loss continued to improve. Rather than increase capacity or simply train longer, I wanted to understand what was limiting useful development learning. Short-horizon financial targets are extremely noisy, so an easy early direction can dominate before the recurrent representation has had much time to develop. My working principle became to keep the early dynamics receptive and well-conditioned long enough for useful structure to form, while still allowing the network to specialise later.

The model itself was deliberately simple: one year of history compressed into 14 ordered temporal buckets, processed oldest-to-newest by a 64-unit GRU, with only the final hidden state entering a \(64\to32\to1\) head. Training used 2000–2015 and development 2016–2019.

Too much carry, too early

The first defect was excessive carry into the final prediction:

\[ h_t=\underbrace{z_t\odot h_{t-1}}_{\text{carry}} +\underbrace{(1-z_t)\odot n_t}_{\text{write}}. \]

Removing only the final carry term from the trained model increased development Rank IC from 0.0156 to 0.0193. Earlier recurrence and candidate computation were left intact, so the problem was the direct terminal route, not historical information itself.

Chrono initialisation was the natural suspect: it began around \(z\approx0.865\), imposing strong persistence before the hidden state had learned a useful representation. Turning it off started the model from \(z=0.5\). At selection, the final update gate fell from about 0.81 to 0.52 and carry's share of score variance from 0.53 to 0.28, while the useful write component was essentially unchanged. The model also trained for substantially longer, with gross Sharpe rising from 1.25 to 2.19 and Rank IC from 0.0169 to 0.0204. Carry then increased gradually during training: the intervention did not remove memory, it made memory learned.

Stronger memory became too narrow

Removing chrono initialisation substantially reduced the carry problem, but exposed a second one. For the oldest history, long-range transport magnitude \(\|P_s\|_F\) grew by roughly an order of magnitude while stable rank fell from about 3.9 to 1.4 before partially recovering. The GRU was transmitting more history, but the transport was becoming increasingly dominated by its leading singular direction.

The concentration arose through composition. Since \(P_s=J_TJ_{T-1}\cdots J_{s+1}\), the individual one-step Jacobians \(J_t\) did not need to become rank one, and they did not; instead, their dominant directions increasingly aligned across steps, repeatedly amplifying the same modes.

First intervention: recurrent weights

Because the recurrent matrices help shape every local Jacobian, I first tested whether their learned geometry was actually driving the transport bottleneck. I froze the chrono-off model at the trough and replaced only those matrices by their nearest orthogonal counterparts, with no retraining. Stable rank recovered from 1.41 to 3.69, close to the 3.88 initialisation reference. That made recurrent-weight geometry a concrete upstream intervention target.

I added a temporary normalised-isotropy penalty,

\[ R_W=\sum_g\frac1H \left\| \frac{W_{hg}^{\top}W_{hg}} {\operatorname{tr}(W_{hg}^{\top}W_{hg})/H} -I \right\|_F^2 . \]

The penalty changed the intended mechanism: recurrent spectral departure fell 0.322 → 0.094, successive-Jacobian alignment 18.1× → 13.9×, and the transport stable-rank trough improved 1.36 → 1.49. But predictive performance was essentially unchanged. That was still useful evidence: the bottleneck was mechanically controllable, but it was not by itself the limiting source of prediction quality. The penalty therefore acted as an early conditioning brake, not as a permanent preference for orthogonality.

Second intervention: the transport itself

The weight brake acted only on an upstream ingredient of the full Jacobian product, so I next conditioned the composed transport directly:

\[ R_T=\frac1{|\mathcal S|} \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\frac1H \left\| \frac{P_s^\top P_s} {\operatorname{tr}(P_s^\top P_s)/H} -I \right\|_F^2 . \]

Again the pressure was temporary: preserve several viable transport directions while the representation formed, then remove the constraint and allow specialisation. At matched update 378, long-horizon raw transport PR was 2.67 without the penalty versus 4.69 with it. Score-used PR moved in the same direction, though by less, and the trajectories partially reconverged once the penalty disappeared. The intervention therefore opened additional transport capacity without forcing the final model to remain isotropic.

Some of that change survived into the selected model: long-horizon raw transport PR remained 2.51 → 2.87, while score-used PR increased 1.84 → 1.97. The final model also remained economically strong, with gross Sharpe 2.27 → 2.36 and essentially unchanged turnover. The claim is not that broader transport causes higher returns; it is that the diagnosed bottleneck could be controlled directly, part of the extra capacity became score-relevant, and doing so did not sacrifice final predictive performance.

What it bought

The final model keeps the same 15,361-parameter architecture and original readout; what changed was its initialisation and training geometry. The table is therefore a full-recipe before/after comparison, not a single causal ablation: weight decay and the later stopping policy also changed. Chrono-off produced the clearest predictive gain; the transport interventions primarily changed conditioning.

What remains unresolved

The final model still had measurable carry headroom: removing only terminal carry increased selected development IC from 0.0186 to 0.0196. The original problem was reduced, not eliminated. One possibility is that the remaining tension lies partly at the readout, where carry and write are merged before the nonlinear head. I left that question open rather than adding another architectural intervention.

The broader lesson was to keep three questions separate: what the recurrence can transmit, what the score actually uses, and what ultimately improves prediction.
